# Writing a CTF solve people can learn from

The requested style reference is [Orange Tsai's *The Art of PHP*](https://blog.orange.tw/posts/2025-08-the-art-of-php-ch/) and its [full English Phrack edition](https://phrack.org/issues/72/the-art-of-php-my-ctf-journey-and-untold-stories_md). It pairs first-person context with precise technical mechanisms, short labeled examples, a question that leads into each discovery, and credit to earlier work. The workbench uses those broad teaching choices in an **original** voice. It does not copy Orange Tsai's personal history, language, jokes, diagrams, or prose.

For a single challenge, a strong arc is: **the constraint → the clue → the mechanism → the decisive check → a reproducible solve → what the result actually proves**. A surprising detail earns a paragraph only if it caused a concrete change in approach. Put the exact evidence beside the claim it supports. A command list without the reason for each step teaches little; a long story without a check cannot establish the solve.

The `ctf-writeup` skill writes in the user's point of view from supplied notes. It tracks actions the user performed, agent-run local checks, teammate observations, and outside research during editing. First-person scenes or emotions require a real note from the user. The published narrative should state the checks that happened with their output, without a “Check and limit” paragraph, agent-attribution disclaimer, or inventory of absent checks. Claim challenge-service acceptance only when evidenced. Respect event embargoes and flag-sharing rules before publishing. Use [the writeup template](../templates/ctf-writeup.md) as a flexible draft, and [the CTF workflow](workflow.md#ctf-track) for evidence collection while solving.
